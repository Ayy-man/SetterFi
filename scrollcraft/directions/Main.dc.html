The structural fork

Two ways to build the same argument.

Same locked header, same copy, same proof. What changes is whether the scroll cuts between scenes or travels through one place. Every other decision on the build follows from this one, so it is worth picking deliberately.

A

Distinct scenes

Six frames, six devices, a hard cut between each.

Why it wins. Six devices means six surprises, and the gaps between them are free. It survives a visitor who scrolls fast, and every act can be cut or reordered without rebuilding the page.

The tradeoff. It reads as a very good marketing page rather than a place. Nothing carries the visitor from one act to the next except the argument itself, so the copy has to be strong.

Why it wins. The ground travels from night to morning under the visitor's hand, so the product's whole claim is felt before a word of it is read. Nobody leaves in the middle of a move.

The tradeoff. One device stretched over a whole page, so if the move is not beautiful there is nowhere to hide. Without generated footage it has to be built in DOM and canvas, which is real engineering, and a fast scroller gets a blur instead of an argument.
